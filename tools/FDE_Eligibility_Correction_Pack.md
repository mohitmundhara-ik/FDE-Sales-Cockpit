## 1. What is actually wrong

I described this earlier as the brochure "excluding" QA/SDET, Frontend, Mobile and Security. That was wrong, and the distinction matters.

**Nothing is excluded. Those profiles are simply never mentioned.** There is no exclusion clause anywhere. There is a list of nine profiles, and if a reader is not on it they conclude the programme is not for them — which is an omission behaving like a rejection.

| Source | What it says |

| **US landing page** (`Who This Is Built For`) | Four grouped lines: Customer Engineers, Solutions Architects, Solutions Engineers · Software Engineers, DevOps/SRE, Platform Engineers · MLEs, Data Engineers, Data Scientists · Tech Leads, TPMs, EMs who still code |

**Absent from all three:** QA / SDET / Test leadership, Data Analysts and BI, Mobile engineers, Frontend engineers, Security engineers.

## 2. The rule the copy should follow

The prerequisites already do the gatekeeping, and they do it correctly:

> Solid understanding of software-engineering principles and practices · Python proficiency, SQL fluency a strong advantage · Comfort with at least one cloud provider · **Prior AI/ML background is NOT required.**

That is the real bar. It is about **capability**, not job title. The `Best Suited For` list should therefore be **illustrative, not definitional** — and it should say so, because right now a reader reasonably treats it as definitional.

## 3. Replacement copy — KYP `Best Suited For`

> ### Best Suited For
>
> The FDE course is built for engineers who want to design, build and deploy real AI systems inside customer environments, not just demos. **What matters is your engineering foundation, not your job title.** If you meet the prerequisites below, the profiles here are examples rather than a checklist.
>
> **Backend, Fullstack and Platform Engineers** — Engineers who want to go beyond calling APIs and build real AI applications that ship to customers.
>
> **Senior Software Engineers** — Experienced engineers who want to add the customer side of AI delivery to their skill set: scoping problems with customers, shipping AI products and owning them from start to finish.
>
> **Machine Learning Engineers** — ML engineers who want to step out of the model lab and into the role that builds, deploys and runs AI systems for real customers.
>
> **Data Engineers, Data Scientists and Analysts** — Data professionals moving into AI delivery, applying pipeline and domain skills to build AI applications customers actually use. *(added: Analysts)*
>
> **Customer Engineers and Solutions Architects** — Engineers at cloud providers like Google, AWS, Azure, Salesforce, Snowflake and Databricks who already work with customers and want to add real AI engineering depth.
>
> **Solutions Engineers at AI Startups** — Engineers who want to grow from running demos and pilots into shipping and running real AI deployments at customer sites.
>
> **DevOps, Platform and SRE Engineers** — Cloud, infrastructure and reliability engineers who want to specialise in running AI systems in production.
>
> **QA, SDET and Test Engineering Leads** — *(new)* Engineers whose reliability and evaluation instinct transfers directly. Evaluations, guardrails and production quality for non-deterministic AI systems are a core part of the FDE role, and this is where testing backgrounds start ahead.
>
> **Mobile, Frontend and Client Engineers** — *(new)* Product engineers who want to move from one surface to owning the full AI system behind it, deployed in the customer's environment.
>
> **Security, Identity and Platform Governance Engineers** — *(new)* Engineers who already live in the review that stops most AI deployments. Enterprise AI ships through SSO, RBAC, audit and data residency, and that fluency is scarce.
>
> **Technical Program Managers Who Still Code** — TPMs who want to get back into building, adding AI engineering depth and direct customer delivery experience.
>
> **Engineering Managers and Tech Leads Who Still Code** — Hands-on leaders who want to get back into building AI systems, or lead a team of AI Engineers and FDEs with the right design vocabulary and playbook.
>
> **Prerequisites**
> - Solid understanding of software-engineering principles and practices
> - Python proficiency. SQL fluency is a strong advantage
> - Comfort with at least one cloud provider (AWS, GCP or Azure)
> - Prior AI/ML background is **not** required
>
> **Not sure you fit?** The prerequisites are the real bar. If you write Python unaided, have deployed something to a cloud you did not own, and want to work with customers rather than only tickets, book a call and we will map your specific background.

**Three additions, one broadening, one framing line.** Nothing removed.

## 5. Why each addition is defensible

**QA / SDET / Test** — The strongest of the three. FDE evaluations and guardrails are quality thinking applied to non-deterministic systems, and evaluation appears in 57% of Core FDE postings in the August 2026 India scrape. A Test Manager has already enrolled. The FDE Scorecard rates SDET/QA at 48% of the bar and names the reliability instinct as a genuine edge.

**Mobile / Frontend** — Weakest on paper: production breadth is narrower, and the tool models a mobile background at 36 of 100 against 44 for backend. But that is a gap, not a disqualification, and the prerequisites already screen for what matters. Listing them with honest framing is better than silent omission.

**Security / Identity** — Security and governance appears in 66% of Core FDE postings and 73% at Lead and Principal. It is the block most engineers have never touched, and security engineers arrive holding it. Scarce and genuinely valuable.

**Data Analysts** — A Data Analyst has already enrolled. Domain expertise as the foundation with AI engineering layered on top is exactly how that call was positioned, and it converted.

---

## 6. What must not change

- **Prerequisites stay exactly as they are.** They are the real gate and they are correct. Widening the illustrative list must not read as lowering the bar.
- **No placement or outcome language** anywhere near this section.
- Do not claim these profiles are *equally* ready. They are not, and the Scorecard already says so. The honest line is that the prerequisites decide it.
